Fluxgates are a type of large machinery that utilize fluxes to travel between different worlds, without traversing through space.

#### Mechanism

Through the assumption that there is a flux where an alternate version of the desired world would reside where the fluxgate is right now, a [[Seer]] combs through the infinite fluxes to find one (the process can take anywhere from a few years to a century). 

Nevertheless, once found, the flux is linked to fluxgate, whereby one can step through the fluxgate to arrive at the flux. 

The link between the fluxgate and the flux is the only thing that keeps the flux in existence; without it, the flux reverts back to a mere possibility. However, foreign entities not native to the flux do not revert with it, instead being transferred to where the world would be in there own flux.

So, how a fluxgate works is:

	Figure A - original flux
	Figure B - flux of alternate world
	Figure X - person
1) X steps through fluxgate
2) X is in B
3) The fluxgate unlinks, unmaking B and sending X to the desired world in A
4) Fluxgate relinks, recreating B

But how does one get back to their original world? Herein lies the danger.

There are a number of issues with the fluxgate, namely how one gets back to their original world and the possibility of being transferred to an unsafe location when X goes to A. 

For the first, once a fluxgate is deemed safe for travel, another Seer (Seers are needed to initially connect the fluxgate with the flux), along with multitudes of automatons, engineers, machinery, and materials, are sent to the desired world, where they are tasked with creating fluxgate that goes back to the original world. Failure to do so would leave them stranded on a foreign planet, with investors, corporations, and/or nations facing immense loss. 

For the second, once Seers have found the appropriate flux, they are tasked with calculating safe locations for when X goes to A.
