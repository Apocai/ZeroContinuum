
##### **Personality (Outer):**

Apocanyser is a lazy and lackadaisical man, yet buried within him is a honest and kind heart  that never hesitates to help others, all the while masking his embarrassment through an onslaught of complaining and insults. It is apparent even to other that he has a strong set of morals he must follow, ones that have earned him high-esteem in the eyes of others.

Yet any compliments are brushed of, not with modesty but as though such words don't even reach him. Others describe it as a sort of numbness with the way he lives, as though he is not truly present in the moment, but detached to everything but himself. It is as though he is watching the world through a screen, able to appreciate what he sees but never truly be in it. 
Such thoughts are only natural, for Apocanyser lacks a soul. He cannot see what other, "real" people see so easily, and can only imagine what such a life might looks like. 

But, beyond that, it may simply be that he is that sort of man. What happens outside is of no concern too him, so long as it does not go against his code. For,  the battle Apocanyser faces was always, only, against himself.

#####  **Personality (Inner):**

The child was never happy. There was nothing that captivated him, nothing that interested him, and nothing that made his want to continue living. At the time, his sole reason for living was to not make his [[Daemonium Theana|guardian's]] efforts caring for him go to waste. 

Even as he adapted to his soulless body, this did not change. The young boy failed to see any beauty in life, and wondered for what reason he existed. And that was when he met Nerathys.

Through her, he saw a blinding light that was the polar opposite of him. He understood her to be similarly a blank state, yet she fully embraced her emptiness as a way to absorb everything. The boy tried emulating her, only to realize he couldn't share her passion. And, noticing such a laughable sight, she told him: 

*Fufufu. How pathetic you are, foolish brother. Its so simple, and yet that little brain of yours couldn't think of it. Fine then. I shall tell you, for the sole reason that it will haunt your life forever. If you lack passion, then steal it. If you wish for vibrancy, than drain such color from others. After all, this world was built upon such laws, no?*

At the time he couldn't understand what she was saying, yet her words resonated within him. With him having nothing else to do, he continued along the path of strength hoping the meaning of her words would become clear to him.

***
So he trained for that reason alone. So that he may find the answer to his conflict. So that he may fill his hollow self. But of course, such peace is never easily obtained. 

So what must he do? How must one, born without talent, succeed? He struggled for years with this. He witnessed as his genius younger sister progressed faster than he ever had. He watched in frustration as Nerathys broke every limit he thought possible.

And he arrived at an answer: his immortality. He could push himself without mercy and restraints, and his body would heal. It would exact a toll on him, yes. He would surely suffer as he tore his body apart again and again to break past his mediocrity. But he must. For that is what is required of him. 

Apocanyser was not inherently a dedicated or motivated person. He hates to live in discomfort, and would rather do nothing than strain himself. But he would do so anyway. His own thoughts were irrelevant before the rules he chained himself to. Such is why others believe him to be solipsistic. For, to him, his own struggle is of the highest importance.

##### Killing:

Before he killed, he believed it to be a feat done without hesitancy or fear. He had lived among those who had slain enough to leave behind an ocean of blood, and thus believed he should strive to match them. 

But one day, when he had first killed another man, an emotion arose with him, not guilt or disgust, but an almost drug-like clarity. It was as though he had lived with a fog clouding his mind, one that was blown away in this ordeal, leaving behind something akin to enlightenment.

He felt he could do anything, be anything, kill anything. He was no longer burdened by himself, and could finally be free. 

And yet the moment faded, and Apocanyser was left haunted. That was no possession, no alter ego. The thoughts he had, they were his alone, akin to arriving at the solution to a long attempted math problem. And the minute he snapped back to reality, the solution went with it. Which was his true self, the man he believed himself to be, or the man he became after murder?

He thought to himself: to act in that state would be to surrender, to become a slave to himself, and that is the one bit of freedom that he will never let be taken away. So, a rule was hammered into his core, a rule he must follow. 

*He will not kill, because to kill is to forsake himself*.

But was such a truth merely another way of running away?

### Oneness of Mind and Body (True Inner):

^1556c1
At his core, a question would plague him. Bound as he was by his morals and rules, was he not already a slave? How could he yearn to have freedom from all his struggles, to ascend beyond on them, if he was not willing to break the bonds that bound him?

And it is only through murder that he briefly arrives, no, becomes the answer: **Oneness of Mind and Body**. For through killing, his entire being is concentrated on one act: the act of murder. Hesitation, doubt, agony - any and all such conflict is wiped clean, leaving behind only the singular moment of triumph.

Yes, it came back to what Nerathys told him, so long ago: that to find meaning he must steal it. He believed himself too weak to find meaning in his life, too weak to chase the joy of freedom. At its core, it was naught but his own ego holding him back. 

Realizing he is a weak being, a pathetic man who needs to fuel such a mundane thing to truly live, he will pit himself against the living, against those with purpose. And with their death, he will prove himself the stronger, the worthier, and bring kindling to his ego. And with that will one day bring him freedom. The freedom gained only by acknowledging himself and breaking free of his self-imposed bindings
